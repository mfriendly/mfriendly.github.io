ABOUT

I develop machine learning methods for prediction and decision-making in dynamic environments. My work spans forecasting under distribution shift, counterfactual estimation of intervention effects, and the selection of interventions under resource constraints. Predictions are rarely the end goal: they are inputs to decisions, so I study how models should be built and evaluated when decision quality, not accuracy alone, is what matters.

I work across public health, energy, and economic domains, and my methods draw on graph neural networks, time series models, and large language models. Before my Ph.D., I worked in the Korean asset management industry on fund strategy and alternative investments, and I hold the FRM certification.
